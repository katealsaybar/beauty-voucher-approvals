How to read this
Two ends of the same submission. What she gets is the real email from the build, rendered as it lands on a phone, next to what the page promised her. What we see is what the quiz actually sends us, and what reception would have in front of them when they ring her within two hours.
The gap, in one line. The page promises “a full plan written for you: the exact treatments, your home care, and your rhythm, visit by visit.” The email delivers one line of what-we-heard and a promise that somebody will call. The plan is a phone call, not a written plan. Either the page stops promising it in writing, or the email has to carry it.

Why these four, and why Outlook on Windows is one of them. Client and device are two different questions (Gmail runs on both handsets), so these are four combinations people actually read email in, rather than a row that mixes the two. Outlook on Windows renders through Word, not a browser engine, which is why it is the one that genuinely breaks emails and why it cannot be left out. “It looked fine in Gmail” is how a broken email ships.

So one of two things has to change. Either the email grows a real plan block (recommended treatments, home care, and the visit rhythm, merged from the answers she already gave), or the page stops saying the plan comes in writing and promises the call instead. The first is truer to the doctrine (we diagnose first, and the plan is the product) but it is the bigger build. The second ships this week. Tara's call.
Two smaller things in this email. The footer lists all four salons, which is right for mapping but will read oddly to someone who arrived from a single-emirate voucher page. And the workflow node that sends it is still titled “The Journal” even though the email says Confidence Map. The map and the asset disagree.
Found by building the Outlook pane, and it is not just this email. Every footer sets its text with color:rgba(255,255,255,…) on a near-black background. Outlook on Windows has never supported rgba colour, so it drops the rule and the text falls back to near-black: black on black. The footer, and the unsubscribe link inside it, become unreadable for every client on Outlook desktop. That is not only a look problem: an unsubscribe nobody can find is what gets a sender marked as spam.

All thirteen emails do it, four or five times each. The fix is a solid hex instead of rgba: the same grey, one line per file, no visible change in any other client. Kate can make it in an hour, but it changes thirteen client-facing assets, so it is flagged here rather than done quietly. Switch to Outlook · Windows above to see the full list for this email.

Corrected 8 August, after reading the live form.

One: locale is not the emirate. The live script sets it from the page's language attribute, so it holds en-us for every lead and always will. There is nothing being “thrown away” and nothing to recover. Mapping leads simply have no emirate until she is asked directly, hence the emirate:unknown fallback above.

Two: the quiz is Gravity Forms on WordPress, not a GHL form, and Gravity Forms does not talk to GHL on its own. That hop is no longer a mystery. We checked wp-admin directly: no Webhooks feed, no Zapier feed, and the one plugin built for this job is switched off and, worse, fires on every form on the site with no way to scope it to just this one. So the decision is to build a clean bridge rather than revive that plugin.
The build, in order. One, create all nineteen custom fields in GHL before anything else, because a field that does not exist when the form posts drops its value with no error anywhere: pain as Single Line Text, condition as Text, not Number. Two, a Make.com scenario: a Custom Webhook trigger fed by Gravity Forms' own Webhooks add-on (already installed, free), a step that splits pain into its own tags and folds in route / tier / emirate:unknown, then GHL's “Update or Create Contact”, matched on phone number, so a returning client is updated rather than duplicated. Three, submit one test entry and read it at both ends: the Gravity Forms entry list and the resulting GHL contact. Four, the contact note in reading order, written at the same moment as the fields, before the contact-centre task. Keys, types and the note layout are in docs/CONFIDENCE-MAPPING-FIELD-SPEC.md; the Make scenario is drawn module by module in the workflow map: open automations.html and pick the Make.com bridge lane, where every module carries the settings to build it from.
Why this matters more than it looks. The page's whole promise is “we diagnose first and recommend second, so nothing in your plan is guessed.” If reception rings her without her answers in front of them, the first human contact guesses. That is the one thing the page says we do not do.
