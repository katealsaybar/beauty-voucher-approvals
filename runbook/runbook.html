Runbook: what to do when it goes wrong

Not a review document. This is the page you open while something is failing, so it is ordered by urgency rather than by workflow.
1 · Kill switch 2 · Error gates 3 · Failure modes 4 · Who to wake 5 · Still to build
1

The kill switch

Four switches, in this order. The order is the whole point: stop the messages before you stop anything else, because a message already sent cannot be recalled and a payment already taken does not need to be.

The rule that decides everything below: kill the messaging, never the money. Stripe stays on. A client who pays during an incident is a client who gets tagged late, which the “already placed your credit” note in the emails now covers. A client who cannot pay is revenue you do not get back, plus a support conversation you created yourself.
  1. 1

    Pause the GHL workflow that is misbehaving

    GoHighLevel → Automation → Workflows → the workflow → toggle Published off
    Stops every step for every contact still in that workflow, immediately, including waits that have not resolved.
    Does not stop the other two workflows, anything already handed to the email or WhatsApp provider in the last few minutes, or the Make scenarios that feed it.
    Pause the one that is wrong. Pausing all three when only Lead Nurture is misfiring stops confirmation emails to women who have just paid, which turns one problem into two.
  2. 2

    Turn off the Make scenario that feeds it

    Make → Scenarios → the scenario → the ON/OFF toggle. Scenario 1 is the quiz bridge, scenario 2 is the Stripe bridge.
    Stops new contacts being created, tagged and noted. Incoming webhooks queue rather than vanish, so nothing is lost while it is off.
    Does not stop Stripe taking payments, and does not stop the GHL workflows running for contacts already in them.
    Turning off scenario 2 has a cost you have to plan for. A woman who pays while it is off gets no voucher:paid tag, so she gets no Welcome Pack and no confirmation. Write down the time you switched it off. Every Stripe payment between then and switching it back on needs tagging by hand, in the order in section 3.
  3. 3

    Turn off the Gravity Forms feed, only if the quiz is the source

    WordPress → Forms → form 21 → Settings → Webhooks → toggle the feed off
    Stops quiz submissions reaching Make at all.
    Does not stop the form working. She still submits, the entry is still stored in WordPress, and it can be replayed later. Nothing she typed is lost.
    Rarely the right switch. Turning off scenario 1 in Make achieves the same thing and keeps the queue, so reach for this only if the feed itself is what is broken, for example if it is firing twice per submission.
  4. 4

    Tell the desk before you tell anyone else

    The branch WhatsApp groups, and Belle
    Stops reception improvising. If clients are calling about an email they should not have had, the desk needs one sentence to say, and it should not be a sentence they invented under pressure.
    Does not stop anything technical. This is the step everyone skips and it is the one clients actually feel.
    The sentence: “That went out in error and we are fixing it now. Your credit is safe on your account and nothing has changed.” Do not promise a time unless you know one.

Before you switch anything back on

  1. Do not amend a live workflow and republish it. GHL does not retro-apply a new node: everyone already past the insertion point never sees the fix, so Wednesday's correction silently misses everyone who entered on Monday. Build the corrected version as a separate v2, let v1 drain, then point the trigger at v2.
  2. Count what was missed. Stripe payments during a scenario-2 outage, and quiz submissions during a scenario-1 outage. Both are countable, and both need a backfill list before you turn anything on.
  3. Backfill with a catch-up, never by re-running the arc. Nobody receives Day 0 twice. A small workflow scoped to the affected tag only.
2

Error gates in Make

A Make module with no error handler stops the whole run at that module. The question is never “should this have a handler”, it is “if this module fails, is the run still worth finishing?”

One principle decides every row below: break on identity and money, ignore on convenience. If the module carries who she is or what she paid, a failure has to stop the run and be retried, because everything after it is wrong without it. If the module is a nicety, a failure must not cost her the parts that matter.

Two settings to turn on before either scenario goes live

Allow storing of incomplete executions Scenario settings. A failed run is parked with its data intact and can be resumed once the cause is fixed, instead of being lost. Without it, “break” means “discard”.
Sequential processing Scenario settings. Runs execute one at a time in order. Two payments landing in the same second cannot race each other into creating two contact records.
Both are off by default. Turning them on afterwards does not recover anything that already failed.

Scenario 1 · the quiz bridge

ModuleGateWhy
1 · Custom webhooknone A trigger cannot have a handler. Its protection is the 200 response in module 7.
2 · Set variablesnone Pure computation on data already in hand. If this fails the payload is malformed and the run should stop loudly.
3 · Create or update contactBreak Identity. Everything below needs the contact ID. Break parks the run with her answers intact so it can be resumed; ignore would silently drop a real lead who filled in nineteen questions.
4 · Add contact tagsBreak Identity. route: and tier: are what the contact centre and the kit packers filter on. An untagged contact is invisible to both.
5 · Create a noteIgnore Convenience. The note is a reading aid. Losing it must not cost her the task in module 6, which is the thing with a two-hour clock on it.
6 · Create a taskBreak This is the two-hour first-contact promise. A dropped task is a woman who completed a nineteen-question map and never heard from anyone.
7 · Webhook responseIgnore Convenience. The 200 is courtesy to WordPress. The work is already done by the time this runs, and Gravity Forms does not retry on a missing response.

Scenario 2 · the Stripe bridge

ModuleGateWhy
1 · Watch eventsnone A trigger. Stripe retries a failed webhook delivery on its own schedule, which is the safety net here.
2 · Set variablesnone Decodes the payment link into an emirate. A failure here means an unrecognised link, which must stop the run rather than guess.
3 · Create or update contactBreak Money and identity. She has paid. Losing this run means a paying client with no record of the purchase in GHL.
4 · Add contact tagsBreak Money. Emirate and tier. The Welcome Pack's first step reads the emirate; without it the confirmation renders with two blank buttons.
5 · Create a noteIgnore Convenience. The receipt in till language. Useful, not load-bearing.
6 · Add tag voucher:paidBreak The starting gun. This tag ends the nurture and starts the Welcome Pack. If it fails after modules 3 and 4 succeeded, she is a paying client still being sold to. Break, and put this one on a notification.
One Make setting to check that is not a gate. Extra credits auto-purchase is off on the Core plan. That is the right setting, since it stops a runaway loop spending money without asking. It also means that if 10,000 credits are ever exhausted the scenarios stop rather than overspend. At roughly 7 and 6 credits a run that is about 1,400 quiz submissions or 1,600 payments in a cycle, which this window will not reach. Worth knowing rather than worth changing.
3

Failure modes, and the contingency for each

Ordered by how much it costs a client, not by how likely it is. Every one of these is silent: none of them raises an error anywhere, which is why each row has a what you would see rather than a log to check.

Worst

A woman who has paid keeps getting the sell emails

What you would see
A complaint, or Belle noticing a client on the nurture list who is already on the paid list. Nothing in any log.
What it means
Two contact records for one woman. Scenario 1 matches on phone, scenario 2 matches on email, so a quiz done on one and a payment made on the other never join up. Her voucher:paid tag landed on record B while the nurture runs on record A, and the goal that ends the arc is on A.
Contingency now
The “already placed your credit” note is in all seven pre-purchase emails, so she is told to ignore it rather than left to conclude we do not know she paid. That is a cushion, not a fix.
The fix
Match on email in both scenarios, or add a search-by-email-then-phone step. Merge the duplicate pair in GHL by hand and re-apply the tags to the survivor.
Who
Kate builds. Belle spots it first.
Worst

A confirmation email arrives with two blank buttons

What you would see
Open the sent copy of Email 1. Where the two salon buttons should be, there is nothing, or the words render as empty. No error, no bounce.
What it means
An unpopulated GHL merge field renders blank rather than failing. The emirate was never written before the Welcome Pack read it, so {{branch_a_name}} and {{branch_b_name}} came back empty.
Cause, almost always
A hand-applied voucher:paid that arrived before the emirate, tier and receipt. See the manual sequence below.
Contingency
Pause the Welcome Pack. Set the fields on the affected contacts. Send Email 1 to them as a one-off rather than re-running the workflow, which would send Email 2 and 3 again as well.
Who
Whoever applied the tag. Kate re-sends.
Bad

A manual tag applied out of order, or late

What it is
Tabby is a unique payment link per client, verified and tagged by Belle. A till payment is tagged by the branch, within the day. Both are hand-applied, and both reach the Welcome Pack the moment voucher:paid lands.
The trap
voucher:paid is not a label, it is a trigger. Applied first, it starts the Welcome Pack before the fields the Welcome Pack reads have been written, and the result is the blank-button failure above.
The sequence, and it is not optional
1 · emirate and emirate_source 2 · tier:[x] and branch:[x] 3 · the amount, as a note 4 · voucher:paid, last and on its own
The real fix
Do not hand anyone a tag. A short GHL internal form with four questions applies all four in the right order, and the ordering trap stops existing rather than being documented. An hour of work, and nobody has to remember a sequence at a busy till.
The late-tag cost
“Within the day” means a woman who pays at 10am can get her confirmation at 7pm. That is survivable and the emails now cover it, but the shorter that gap is the less this page matters.
Who
Belle for Tabby. Branch reception for till. Kate builds the form.
Bad

A refunded voucher keeps receiving the Welcome Pack

Will this even happen
Yes, and the T&Cs are what create it. Term 3 holds the credit to the emirate it was bought in and then says that if she has bought the wrong one we “will do our best to put it right”. That sentence invites a refund conversation in writing. Refunds are not an edge case we are importing, they are a path the document already promises her.
What it means today
Nothing watches charge.refunded. The tag stays, so the arc continues and she keeps being welcomed to a voucher she no longer holds.
The part that is not obvious
Removing voucher:paid afterwards does not pull her out. The goal fired once and she is already inside the workflow. Untagging her changes nothing.
The build, three parts
1 · Stripe module watches charge.refunded too 2 · Router path: remove voucher:paid, add voucher:refunded, note the amount and date 3 · Welcome Pack gets a hard exit on voucher:refunded
Part 3 is the one that actually stops the messages, and it is the one that gets forgotten because parts 1 and 2 feel like the work. Build it the same way voucher:redeemed already works: a goal node with the exit behaviour, not a filter.
Partial refunds
Stripe fires charge.refunded for a partial refund as well. If we ever refund part of a tier, treat it as a full exit and have a human re-place the credit by hand. Do not try to compute a remaining balance in a workflow.
Contingency until it is built
Belle tells Kate on the day of any refund, and the contact is removed from the workflow by hand. That works precisely because the volume is low; it stops working the moment it is not.
Who
Kate builds all three parts. Belle reports.
Bad

She was shown one emirate and paid through the other

What it means
Her tag says one emirate, her Stripe link says another. Under term 3 the credit is held to the emirate it was bought in, so the payment wins on the data. It is never a silent re-tag: she was shown one voucher and holds another.
Contingency
Hold both regional sends. Raise the mismatch task. A human calls within two hours, before her first redemption, which is the window term 3 invites her to use.
How hard the call is
Read emirate_source. picker means she chose it herself and was sold against that choice, so escalate. phorest means we inferred it and were wrong, so confirm and move on.
Not a conflict
A Saadiyat client paying through the Khalifa City link. Saadiyat has no working Stripe, so that is expected. The emirate agrees, nothing holds.
Who
Contact centre calls. Kate sets the severity rule.
Planned

Meta has not approved the emirate-ask template

What you would see
The step sits in the workflow with nothing to send. No warning, no bounce.
Contingency, already designed
Hold the unknown-emirate cohort out of the Day 0 WhatsApp entirely and send them the neutral email only. Email needs no approval. The email's WhatsApp link opens a chat, and once she writes to us first the 24-hour service window lets us reply with no template at all.
The hole in that fallback
It only works if somebody answers. The window is 24 hours from her message, not from ours, and the reply has to be human. Confirm who is on the inbox and at what hours before relying on this.
Timing
Not the emergency it was written as. The window opens 17 August and the nurture arc does not start until 15 September, so there is a month of runway. Submit early anyway: a rejection restarts the clock.
Who
Kate submits. Whoever holds the WhatsApp inbox answers.

And responsibility is not the same as access. And 971 58 155 9679, the number in the footer of all thirteen emails, is live: it is the line for prospects who have no contact with a salon yet, which is exactly the audience the pre-purchase emails are written for. 5

Still to build, with the how

Three items that are cheap now and expensive later, because each one is something you are told not to do once the window is open. They are here rather than in the build specs because each exists to make an incident survivable.

Built 10 Aug 1 · The nine Stripe links, in a data store rather than in a scenario

Why it could not wait. Section 1 says never amend a live scenario mid-window. Had the nine payment link IDs stayed hardcoded in a Set multiple variables module, correcting one mis-mapped link would have meant doing exactly the thing that rule forbids. The two rules collided, and the collision only had a cheap answer before the window opened.

Live in Make now. Team My Team · data store voucher_payment_links (id 160293) on data structure Voucher payment links (id 524304). Five text fields: link_id as the key, emirate, branch, tier, note. All nine rows are in.

It caught something on the way in. Motor City's three links run …0VO1G, …0VO1F, …0VO1E for Dip Your Toes, Season of You and All-In VIP Year: reversed against the other two branches, which run 01, 02, 03 in tier order. A mapping written from the pattern rather than from the table would have had all three Motor City tiers wrong, and the failure would have been silent, because a wrong tier still routes to the right emirate. The reversal is written into the note field on those rows so the next person does not re-derive it.

branch is deliberately empty on the three Abu Dhabi rows. Saadiyat has no working Stripe, so its clients pay through the Khalifa City link. A payment there means Abu Dhabi, not Khalifa City, and an empty field is the honest answer where a guess would be a wrong one.

  1. Still to do, and it needs the scenario to exist first. There is no Stripe bridge scenario in this Make account yet: the two Wellness Voucher scenarios are still drawings in automations/automations.html. Nothing here is wired to anything.
  2. When you build scenario 2, read the emirate with a Data store → Search records module filtered on link_id, placed before the contact upsert. Do not re-type the mapping into a variables module.
  3. Handle the miss. If the search returns nothing, the link is one nobody has recorded. Set emirate: unknown, emirate_source: payment and raise the mismatch task. Never guess an emirate from an unrecognised link.
Fixing a wrong link is now editing one row and takes about ten seconds. Nobody opens the scenario, so the mid-window rule is never broken, and anyone can read the routing table without knowing Make.

2 · The refund exit, all three parts

Why it cannot wait. Term 3 promises her we will “do our best to put it right” if she holds the wrong emirate's voucher. That is a refund path written into the T&Cs, so the first refund is a matter of when. Building the exit afterwards does not help the woman it already happened to.

  1. Stripe module: add charge.refunded to the watched events alongside checkout.session.completed.
  2. Router: a second path for the refund event. On it, remove voucher:paid, add voucher:refunded, and write a note with the amount and the date.
  3. GHL Welcome Pack: a hard exit goal on voucher:refunded, built the same way voucher:redeemed already is.
Step 3 is the one that actually stops the messages, and it is the one that gets skipped because the first two feel like the work. Untagging her does nothing on its own: the goal fired once and she is already inside the workflow.

3 · test:seed, applied before the import and not after

Why it cannot wait. The eight test contacts exist to exercise every branch, which means they look exactly like real leads to every filter in the system. Add the exclusion after the import and they have already had the broadcast.

  1. Tag the eight seed contacts test:seed as the first thing you do to them, before they are used for anything.
  2. Exclude the tag in each workflow's entry filters, not in a branch partway down. One condition per workflow, at the top: does not have tag test:seed. An exclusion halfway down still lets a test contact trigger everything above it.
  3. Exclude it in the broadcast segment as well, so it holds even if a workflow filter is ever edited out. Two independent guards, because this one fails silently and publicly.
  4. Never reuse a real client's number or email as a seed. A +test alias and a team number only. A seed that is also a client cannot be excluded without excluding her.
Keep the eight after launch rather than deleting them. They are how you test a fix against the live build without sending anything to a real client, which is the thing section 1 tells you to do and which nothing else on this page makes possible.

4 · Un-require the consent tickbox on form 21

Why it cannot wait. This one is not a campaign item and it is not waiting on anything. The UAE Personal Data Protection Law wants consent that is freely given. Field 5 on Gravity Forms 21 is still marked required, which means she cannot get her Confidence Map without agreeing to marketing. Consent bundled into getting the thing she came for is not freely given, and the page is live now.

  1. WordPress → Forms → form 21 → the consent field → General → untick Required. That is the whole change.
  2. Do not remove the tickbox. The consent itself is what lets us message her at all. What comes off is the requirement, not the question.
  3. Check the submit button still works unticked. Submit the form twice, once with the box ticked and once without, and confirm both entries land.
  4. Expect fewer opt-ins, and take it. What the required box collects is form completion, not consent, so the number it was producing was never the number it looked like.
Also worth doing in the same pass, from the field spec's appendix: the consent label and the submit button both still say Journal, and the page <title> still reads “A diagnosis, not a commitment” against a hero that now says “A direction”. Three strings, one visit.
Two built templates are not in this pack. The Wellness Voucher Reserved and Wellness Voucher — Payment Notification messages exist and were test-sent in July, and neither is in data/whatsapp-templates.json, which carries eight. Two consequences. One: the “is Lead Nurture broadcast-fed or form-fed” question that has blocked the build since 7 August is answered by this. A reserve-and-pay path already exists and already has its own two messages, so Lead Nurture can be broadcast-fed without stranding the women who reserve. Two: the test send shows the tier rendering as a raw field value, vip in lower case, where a client should read All-In VIP Year. That is a test doing its job; it still has to be fixed before the template goes near a client. Both templates need adding to the pack and reviewing like every other asset.
